# TASA Custom Landing Detector — ArduCopter 4.7.2

## Background

The TASA branch of ArduPilot is intended for the large TASA octocopter, which has experienced unreliable automatic landing detection with the standard ArduCopter firmware.

TASA is a 30 kg-class octocopter equipped with 30-inch propellers and T-Motor P80 motors. Its propellers are surrounded by a protective structure made of 16 mm carbon-fibre rods, supported by 40 mm carbon-fibre arms. This peripheral structure also serves as the landing gear.

The unusual landing-gear configuration contributes significantly to the difficulties encountered during landing.

## Identified problems

Several factors have been identified.

### 1. Structural flexibility and vibration

When the drone is on the ground, its heavy central body is effectively suspended by the long carbon-fibre arms rather than supported by rigid landing gear directly underneath it.

This produces a *trampoline effect*, allowing the central body to oscillate.

During touchdown, vibrations generated by the large propellers can be severe. Their frequency content also differs from that of smaller multicopters, with significant lower-frequency components.

These vibrations can prevent the standard landing detector from consistently satisfying its accelerometer-stationarity criterion. Consequently, the flight controller may continue to consider the aircraft airborne even after physical ground contact.

### 2. Landing on uneven terrain

During descent, one corner of the peripheral landing structure may touch the ground before the others, particularly on uneven terrain.

This causes the airframe to tilt slightly while the flight controller is still attempting to maintain a level attitude.

The resulting interaction between the landing structure and the attitude controller can make the final touchdown phase particularly difficult, potentially delaying landing detection and disarming.

### 3. Limitations of the standard landing detector

The standard ArduCopter landing detector combines several conditions, including low motor output, low vertical velocity, attitude constraints, and accelerometer stationarity.

These conditions must remain satisfied for a confirmation period before the aircraft is declared landed.

On the TASA airframe, two conditions can be particularly restrictive: `accel_stationary` may be disturbed by structural vibration and oscillation, while `motor_at_lower_limit` may not remain asserted when the attitude controller continues making thrust corrections after ground contact.

## TASA landing detector modifications

The TASA detector deliberately substitutes **two** standard landing criteria:

| Standard criterion | TASA criterion | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `accel_stationary` | `height_gnd_close` | Uses valid downward-rangefinder ground proximity instead of accelerometer stationarity, which may be unreliable on a vibrating, flexible airframe. The threshold is configurable using `RNGFNDx_GNDCLR` plus `LAND_TASA_MARG`. |
| `motor_at_lower_limit` (`motors->limit.throttle_lower`) | `land_mot_low` | Uses a configurable low collective-throttle-output threshold (`LAND_TASA_MOT`) rather than requiring the motor mixer to assert its lower-limit flag. |

These are not direct touchdown sensors: they are permissive conditions that must be satisfied **together** with the retained checks (`throttle_mix_at_min`, no large angle request or error, low descent rate, and weight-on-wheels where applicable). The rangefinder must also report a valid, positive measurement below the proximity threshold.

The motor-limit substitution is motivated by the TASA airframe: contact on uneven ground may provoke continued attitude corrections, so the standard lower-limit flag may be delayed or intermittent even when collective throttle is already low. This is an engineering hypothesis consistent with mock-up logs, not yet a demonstrated full-size-flight result.

The modifications are implemented in a custom branch based on ArduCopter 4.7.2.

### Additional configuration parameters

**`LAND_DET_TASA`**

Enables the custom TASA landing detector.

- `0`: Standard ArduCopter landing detector.
- `1`: TASA landing detector, provided a downward-facing rangefinder is enabled.

If no downward-facing rangefinder is enabled, the standard detector remains in use.

If a rangefinder is enabled but its measurements are temporarily invalid, the TASA ground-proximity condition cannot be satisfied.

**`LAND_TASA_MARG`**

Additional height margin, in metres, added to `RNGFNDx_GNDCLR` to determine the TASA ground-proximity threshold.

This allows the landing detector to account for the geometry and flexibility of the peripheral landing structure.

The margin must be selected carefully: an excessive value could allow landing confirmation to begin before actual ground contact.

**`LAND_TASA_MOT`**

**Configurable low collective-throttle threshold** used instead of the standard `motor_at_lower_limit` (`motors->limit.throttle_lower`) condition.

The TASA condition is satisfied when `motors->get_throttle_out() < LAND_TASA_MOT`. This is a comparison against the normalized **collective throttle command**, not a requirement for the motor mixer to hit its lower limit. It does **not** measure individual motor RPM, actual thrust, or motor PWM, and is distinct from `MOT_SPIN_MIN`.

On a large multirotor with substantial propeller inertia and a flexible or uneven landing structure, the controller may continue issuing attitude corrections after contact. A configurable collective-throttle threshold can allow the detector to accumulate its confirmation count without waiting for `motors->limit.throttle_lower` to become true.

**Safety:** Increasing `LAND_TASA_MOT` makes the criterion easier to satisfy, potentially including low-throttle flight close to the ground. Tune conservatively, in combination with the rangefinder margin and confirmation time; neither low throttle nor ground proximity alone proves touchdown.

### LiDAR requirements

The TASA landing detector requires a functional, downward-facing rangefinder capable of providing reliable measurements close to the ground.

Candidate sensors include the Benewake TFmini Plus and TF-Luna, preferably connected via I²C for convenient integration with the existing ArduPilot driver. Each installation must be validated for measurement latency, update rate, and reliability under its actual operating conditions.

Unlike the standard detector, which uses a rangefinder-related condition alongside accelerometer stationarity, the TASA detector uses the measured distance to the ground as a primary landing criterion.

The distance must be valid, positive, and below the threshold defined by the rangefinder's ground-clearance setting plus `LAND_TASA_MARG`.

The detector therefore depends on both the rangefinder's measurement accuracy and its response time.

### Shorter landing-confirmation period

Another modification reduces the TASA landing-confirmation period to approximately **0.2 seconds**, compared with the longer confirmation period used by the standard detector.

During this interval, all required TASA landing conditions must remain satisfied continuously. If any condition becomes false, the confirmation counter is reset.

This shorter confirmation period is intended to reduce the time during which the aircraft has physically touched the ground but is still considered airborne by the flight controller.

Such a situation can be problematic on uneven terrain, particularly when wind gusts or structural oscillations cause the attitude controller to react while the landing structure is already in contact with the ground.

The shorter confirmation period is experimental and must be validated carefully to avoid premature landing detection.

## Extended LDET logging

To help investigate landing-detection behaviour, the TASA branch extends the existing ArduCopter `LDET` (land detector) DataFlash log message.

The original `Flags` bitmask and `Count` fields are preserved. Three additional fields expose TASA-specific conditions as individual numeric values, making them easy to plot in Mission Planner without manually decoding bitmasks.

| Field | Values | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `LDET.Flags` | Bitmask | Existing landing-detector conditions and additional TASA flags. |
| `LDET.Count` | Non-negative integer | Number of consecutive detector iterations satisfying the landing criteria; resets when a required condition fails. |
| `LDET.MotLow` | `0` or `1` | The TASA motor-output threshold is satisfied. |
| `LDET.HgtClose` | `0` or `1` | A valid rangefinder measurement is below the TASA ground-proximity threshold. |
| `LDET.TasaMode` | `0` or `1` | The TASA detector is selected. |

The three TASA-specific flags are also represented in `LDET.Flags`:

| Flag | Bit | Decimal mask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `LAND_MOTOR_LOW` | 11 | 2048 |
| `HEIGHT_GND_CLOSE` | 12 | 4096 |
| `LAND_TASA_MODE` | 13 | 8192 |

The additional scalar fields are derived from these flags. They are primarily intended for plotting alongside `RFND.Dist`, `RFND.Stat`, `IMU.AccZ`, and the landing-related events.

**Logging-rate limitation:** the land detector can run at approximately 400 Hz, whereas `LDET` messages are rate-limited to one every 20 ms (50 Hz) and are written when the recorded flags or counter change. A condition that briefly fails between log messages may reset `LDET.Count` without a corresponding visible change in the plotted Boolean fields. Therefore, the logs should not be interpreted as a complete record of every detector iteration.

A possible future diagnostic improvement is to record a latched reset reason whenever the landing-confirmation counter is interrupted. This `ResetReason` feature is **not implemented** in the current version.

## Validation status

The modified landing detector has been compiled successfully for both the Pixhawk 4 and Pixhawk 6C flight controllers.

Initial flight tests have been performed on a smaller TASA mock-up equipped with a downward-facing TFmini Plus LiDAR.

These tests included gentle touchdowns and faster landings involving ground contact and bouncing. Landing detection completed successfully in the observed tests. In the latest tilted-autoland mock-up log, the configurable `MotLow` condition became true before the standard `MOTOR_AT_LOWER_LIMIT` flag, supporting the distinction between the two criteria without proving how the full-size TASA will behave. The extended `LDET` fields were also verified in recorded flight logs.

**The modified detector has not yet been flight-validated on the full-size TASA octocopter.**

In particular, replacing the LiDAR with a Doppler radar requires separate validation because of the radar's measurement latency and its limitations at very low relative velocities. Successful LiDAR-based tests do not establish radar suitability.

The 0.2-second confirmation period and ground-clearance margin must be assessed carefully before use on the full-size aircraft, particularly to avoid premature landing detection during a rebound.
